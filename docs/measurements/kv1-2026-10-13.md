# KV1 — the manager over today's slots: gates, registered before the code (2026-10-13)

`docs/tasks/task-kv-architecture-2026-10.md` §6, KV1. Branch `kv1-2026-10`. This file is the registration; §3 on is
filled in as each gate is read. Machines: the MacBook (arm64, Metal) for the build and the day gates, nobara-pc (amd64,
RTX 2070 SUPER, CUDA) for `TestWindowedKV_*` and the CUDA speed arm.

## 1. What KV1 is, and four choices made before the code

KV1 moves resident prefix-reuse bookkeeping into one manager. It changes no storage, no kernel and no allocation, and
a reply must not change.

1. **The manager is its own package**, `internal/kvmgr`, importing nothing from `decoder` (the task doc's §5.13, an
   owner decision of 2026-10-13). `decoder` re-exports its types under `decoder` names for the backends, and derives
   the geometry from the `Architecture`.
2. **The decoder keeps the names the existing tests hold.** `residentReuseLen`, `residentAcquire`,
   `residentCommitIDs`, `residentForgetIDs`, `pickResidentSlot`, `declineShortLeadReuse` and the three fields of the
   bound sequence (`resIDs`, `resIDsLora`, `resImgBlocks`) stay, as calls into the manager and as the bound
   sequence's record. Twelve test sites assign those fields directly, and the identity gate says the tests pass
   unchanged. The parked slots (`resSlots`, `resCur`, `resTick`), the slot choice, the reuse length, the short-prefix
   guard and the publish/forget transitions are the manager's.
3. **The adapter's id in the key is its load identity** (one id per loaded adapter runtime), which is what rule 4's
   pointer comparison was. A content hash would let a reloaded adapter match, which `TestResidentReuseLen_adapterMustMatch`
   forbids today, and which only a disk tier needs (KV7).
4. **KV1 takes no hit from another sequence.** Slot storage cannot share a row without a copy, and the copy is KV2.
   The index, the reference counts and the LRU are built, published to on completion, and checked by the oracle test;
   reuse stays what it is today: a sequence continuing in its own slot, at token granularity.

### G2. Windowed tests at awkward offsets (day, nobara)

**Amended before any run of it (2026-10-13), after reading the fixtures.** As first registered this named starts of
513 and 1,000 on the suite's fixtures and one equality rule. The three windowed fixtures hold 512 positions at most,
and their head dimensions are 32, 16 and 16, so the fast prefill kernels (head dimension 64 or 128) cannot run on
them at all: on those fixtures every pass is the exact class, and the question KV0 left open (is a windowed *fused*
prefill invariant to a start that is not a multiple of 64?) cannot be asked. The gate is now two parts.

- **G2a, the tiny fixtures (`mistral-tiny-window`, `gemma3-vl-tiny`, `cohere2-tiny`), exact class.** Starts of 1, 63,
  65, 100, 129, 257 and 449, none a multiple of 64, and a prompt of 480 tokens.
  - *Storage:* the prompt prefilled in two calls split at each start, on the windowed resident and on the full-KV one;
    logits compared after each call and through 20 decode steps. Rule: the suite's own, bit-identical.
  - *Invariance:* on the full-KV load of the same windowed model, the prompt split at each start, and in one call at
    chunk widths of 50 and 100, against the prompt in one pass: every layer's K and V rows and the last logits. Rule:
    KV0's (0 elements differ), with KV0's control (one token changed at position 300 shows from 300 on and nowhere
    before).
  - Prediction (0.9): all pass. The exact kernels are bit-identical to decode at every start.
- **G2b, a windowed layer on the fast class.**
  - **Amended a second time, 2026-10-13 16:20, before any reading of it.** As registered this loaded
    Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1 q4_k_m, "every layer a 4,096-token sliding window". Run once by day, it stopped in 16 s on
    its own guard: that GGUF carries no window, so goinfer loads it with full attention on every layer, and nothing was
    read. No checkpoint on nobara has both a real window with a head dimension of 64 or 128 and a KV that fits the 8 GB
    card past its window (Command R7B and Olmo 3 have 4,096-token windows; Olmo 3's KV alone is 5.4 GB at 5,120
    positions).
  - **The instrument now:** on CUDA a layer's window is an argument of every attention launch, read from the layer at
    launch time. The test loads a full-attention model whose head dimension is 128 and sets a 256-token window on every
    layer of the loaded resident before the first forward. The weights were not trained with a window, which does not
    matter here: the question is whether one kernel path gives the same bits for two ways of cutting the same prompt.
    Fixtures: `testdata/llama-attnfa-tiny` (KV0's fixture) by day, and Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B q4_k_m under
    `GOINFER_HEAVY_TESTS`. Full-KV storage at f32, a prompt of 1,000 tokens, the default 512-token floor.
  - **Arms**, each against the prompt in one call: split at 576 and 704 (multiples of 64) and at 600 and 777 (not); one
    call at chunk widths of 448 (a multiple of 64) and 500 (not). Each is recorded as invariant or not, with the lowest
    differing position, as KV0 recorded its arms. Launch counts confirm the fast class ran in every arm.
  - **Control:** before the window is set, the same model at full attention, split at 600: invariant, as KV0 found for
    every split whose two calls are both fast class. A difference under the window is then the window's.
  - **This part measures and does not gate KV1.** KV1 takes no rows from another sequence and changes no kernel. The
    answer belongs to the class map KV2 and KV3 will rely on.
  - Prediction, from the census's inference (0.6): the arms at 576, 704 and chunk 448 are invariant; the arms at 600,
    777 and chunk 500 are not, from the first row of the second pass. After the first reading the test pins what was
    observed.
  - Cost: seconds on the tiny fixture, about 2 minutes on the 1.5B. Read by day under the timing lock; it is no longer
    part of the night job.

### G3. Oracle property test (day, in `internal/kvmgr`)

- **What runs.** A seeded random walk of at least 20,000 operations per seed over 8 seeds: admit, append, publish,
  truncate, fork, release, bind and evict, over 1 to 6 slots, against a plain model that holds each sequence as an
  array of rows.
- **Checked after every operation:** each sequence's block table against the model's rows; each block's reference
  count against the number of sequences whose table holds it; that every published block's content equals the rows
  the model says were published, and that its chain hash equals the hash recomputed from scratch; that the index holds
  exactly the published blocks; that the LRU holds exactly the published blocks nobody references; that an unpublished
  row is never reachable through the index.
- **Rule.** Zero violations. A failure prints the seed and the operation index.
- **The test can go red:** three planted defects in the manager (a publish before completion, a reference not dropped
  on release, an eviction of a referenced block), each shown red once and removed.

### G4. Wrong-hit mutations (day, in `internal/kvmgr`)

- **What runs.** Five adversarial tests, one per key part: adapter, image hash, producer class, KV precision,
  namespace. Each builds two requests that differ only in that part and asserts the second takes no hit from the
  first's blocks.
- **Rule.** Each test passes on the manager as built, and goes red when that part is dropped from the key (a mutation
  switch compiled only into the test binary). Each red is shown once, and the output is kept in this record.

### G5. Speed

Tier: day for G5a, night for G5b.

**G5a, the manager's own cost (in process, day).**
- Instrument: a Go benchmark of one acquire, one publish and one forget on a 20,000-token prompt (Claude Code's first
  request) and on a 512-token one, on each box. It is the cheapest instrument that resolves the question, because the
  manager runs once per request and never inside a decode step.
- Rule: at most 1 ms per request at 20,000 tokens. A lone request's time to first token at that length is seconds, so
  1 ms is under 0.1% of it.

**G5b, served, new ÷ main (night, both boxes).**

*Amended before any run of it (2026-10-13):* the instrument for time to first token and the cost. As first registered
this named `scripts/bench_peer_prefill.py`, which compares goinfer with a peer and cannot run two goinfer builds, and
`scripts/bench_prefill_stall.py`'s solo arm, the noise entry's instrument, is Metal-only. The cost was also
under-estimated.

- Instrument, decode: `scripts/bench_peer.py`, two arms, `goinfer` = this branch's merge candidate and `goinfer_old` =
  `main` at the branch point, same session, restarted cells in counterbalanced quads (`BENCH_ABBA`), no peer arm. One
  request at a time, default flags.
- Instrument, time to first token: `docs/measurements/kv1-2026-10-13/kv1_ttft.py`, which is the stall harness's solo
  arm for either backend and two binaries: a block is one fresh server per arm, in alternating order; a warm request,
  then three prompts of 2,500 words (about 3,900 tokens: a 300-word prompt counted 470 in a smoke of the script; the server's own count is recorded) that differ near their
  start, each timed by the client to the first streamed content. A cell's value is the mean of its three.
- Cells: Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct q4_k_m, decode at depth 128 and time to first token as above;
  Metal on the Mac, CUDA on nobara.
- Noise, from `docs/measurements/noise-registry.md`: nobara CUDA 1.5B / 7B decode sd 0.0081 (`seed-nobara-cuda-goinfer-all-S`,
  same-binary restart pairs, n = 20); Mac Metal decode sd 0.0129 (`metal-r17-e2e-control-crossbuild`, cross-build
  A/A, n = 2); Mac Metal time to first token sd 0.0112 (`metal-ttft-chunked-solo`, n = 18, the stall harness's solo
  arm); CUDA time to first token has no entry, and the Mac's 0.0112 is used.
- `power.py served-decode nobara/cuda/1.5B/d128 --bar 0.99 --paired` -> N = 8 blocks (power 0.855).
  `power.py served-decode mac/metal/1.5B/d128 --bar 0.99 --paired` -> **cannot resolve at N <= 12** (power 0.69 at 12).
  `power.py served-ttft mac/metal/1.5B/d128 --bar 0.99 --paired` -> N = 12 (power 0.809); the same N for CUDA with
  the borrowed sd.
- Blocks: 8 for CUDA decode, 12 for every other cell. Fixed, no early stop.
- Statistic: per cell, the median over blocks of new ÷ old (rate for decode; old ÷ new of the time for time to first
  token, so above 1 is faster in both).
- Rule, per cell: **PASS** when the median is in [0.99, 1.01]. **FAIL** when the median is under 0.99 and every block
  is under 1. **PARKED** otherwise (a median outside the band with blocks on both sides of 1, or above 1.01, for which
  KV1 has no mechanism): the owner decides, with G5a's reading beside it. The Mac decode cells cannot resolve the bar
  at this N and are read on that understanding: a PASS there says the median is in the band, not that 0.99 is excluded.
  Graded by `grade_kv1_speed.py`, beside this record.
- Void: an arm that did not run resident on the named backend (`-require-backend`, `BENCH_REQUIRE_BACKEND=1`); a cell
  that errored; a missing arm in a block.
- Cost: about 115 minutes on the Mac (decode 24 cells a model at about 45 s and 75 s; time to first token 24 cells a
  model at about 35 s and 130 s) and about 75 on nobara; `run-kv1-night.sh`, queued as `kv1-speed-metal`
  and `kv1-speed-cuda`.
- Prediction (0.85): every cell reads in the band. The decode loop is untouched, and the manager adds well under a
  millisecond to a request.

### G3. Oracle property test: PASS

`TestOracle_pool`: 8 seeds of 20,000 operations over pools of 12, 24, 48 and 96 blocks, three roots (base, an adapter,
another namespace), 23 s. Zero violations, and every seed shared a published block at least once.
`TestOracle_slots`: 8 seeds of 4,000 operations over 1 to 4 slots against a per-slot record, with the score checked
against the rules written out a second time. Zero violations.

Three planted defects, each shown red once at the first seeds that met it, and removed:

| planted | the oracle's message |
|---|---|
| `Publish` publishes a block that is half full | "seed 1, operation 4: sequence 0: block 0 at table index 0 is published but the sequence has published only 0" |
| a reference not dropped on release (one table index in five) | "seed 1, operation 195: block 11 has 1 references, 0 sequences hold it" |
| a referenced block left in the cache, so evictable | "seed 1, operation 59: block 5: published true with 1 references, in the LRU true (flag true)" |

One expectation of the oracle itself was wrong on its first run and was corrected: it judged a fork that failed for
want of blocks by the pool's state after the call, when the fork had already given back the blocks it took. It now
computes what the fork needs and what is available before the call.
