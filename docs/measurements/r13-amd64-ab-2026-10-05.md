# R-13 on amd64: each shared input quantized once for the CPU W4A8 projections (nobara), 2026-10-05 (pre-registered)

Audit entry: `docs/tasks/task-recompute-audit.md` section 5, R-13 and R-14, shipped on aikit v1.57.0 and on by default (owner: "it stays on"). The Mac's by-day read on arm64 (`docs/measurements/r13-cpu-ab-2026-10-05.txt`) found no measurable effect (1.5B decode 0.995, 256-token prefill 1.014, the Qwen1.5-MoE slice's decode 1.008) because NEON quantization is too cheap for the repeats to show, and said amd64 was not measured: the fused gate+up path is on by default there (2w quantizations a layer down to 1) and quantization is slower. This is that measurement. **It informs; it does not gate:** R-13 stays on whatever it reads.

## Instrument, written before the run

`TestR13_cpuAB` (`decoder/r13_ab_test.go`) as committed in `317bf919`, unchanged: each input quantized once (the default) against every matmul quantizing its own (`w4a8PreOff`), in-process on one loaded model, int4, one activation scale per row. Decode: a fresh cache per arm, 8 warm-up tokens, then the median of 32 timed steps. Batched prefill (dense models only): a 256-token `forwardN`. A warm-up rep, then 7 reps with the arm order alternating rep by rep, the full logits compared bit for bit every rep (a difference fails the run). Models: `~/models/qwen2.5-coder-1.5b-instruct-q4_k_m.gguf` and `~/models/qwen15-moe-a27b-l4slice` (the harness's defaults, local NVMe, not `/srv/models`). Tier: night (it is a timed A/B and the by-day rule says no timed measurement beyond a one-cell smoke); stopping rule: fixed 7 reps, no early stop; cost basis: about 40 forwards at roughly 55 ms and 8 prefills of 256 tokens per arm per model, plus loads, estimated 6 minutes and queued at 10.

`run-amd64.sh` in the directory of the same name, a binary pre-built from `3db00301` at `~/goinfer-logs/r13/decoder.test` (**amended 2026-10-05, before any run:** the first build, from `575a3768`, predates `9863cb4c`, which makes the fused gate+up honour `w4a8PreOff` on amd64; without it the 1.5B's two arms ran the same gate/up code and only the MoE slice could differ. That binary was discarded unrun, and the instrument, the models, the prediction, the decision rule and its bands are unchanged). The box is `nobara-pc`, amd64 (Zen 2, AVX2, no VNNI), 16 threads; the number printed per phase is the median OFF/ON ratio (above 1 means quantizing once is faster) with the count of reps above 1.

## Prediction

Small. The saved quantizations are of a 1536-wide row (a couple of microseconds each on AVX2): about 3 a layer on the dense 1.5B outside the fused path, and the fused gate+up path's workers quantize in parallel, so the wall-time effect of 2w to 1 is about one quantization a layer, the rest being CPU time taken from the matmul. Roughly 100 repeats a token is about 0.2 ms of a 55 ms token. Prediction: decode **1.00 to 1.01**, prefill 1.00 to 1.02, and the MoE slice (2k+2 down to 1 a layer) the largest, **1.01 to 1.03**.

## Decision rule, written before the result

Per phase, the median OFF/ON ratio over the 7 reps:
- **at least 1.005**, with at least 5 of 7 reps above 1: a measurable win on amd64; record it.
- **in [0.99, 1.005)**: no measurable effect; record it, as on arm64.
- **below 0.99, or at most 2 of 7 reps above 1 with a median below 1.0: ambiguous, parked.** That would say quantizing once costs time on amd64 (the shared block is kept alive across projections, or the fan-out loses something). R-13 stays on; the owner gets the data and decides before the next release, and nobody flips it silently.
- a logit mismatch between the arms in any rep is a defect (the harness fails the run), not a result.

Exploratory in the sense that it is one box and 7 reps; not a served or peer claim.
