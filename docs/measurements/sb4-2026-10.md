# SB4: the loader identity, per-tensor hashes — 2026-10-27

The record for SB4 ([`docs/tasks/task-subsystems-2026-10.md`](../tasks/task-subsystems-2026-10.md) §8 SB4), graded
against gates LI1–LI7, registered there before the code (commit `565c9d755`). Tier 0: tiny fixtures, CPU only, seconds.
Measured on mbp2 (Intel i5, Linux amd64, 16 GB) at goinfer main `145fa05f` plus this branch.

## 1. What it is

- **`decoder/loader_identity.go`.** `loaderIdentity(*Weights)` walks the loaded weights by reflection and lists one
  entry per tensor: its field path (`Layers[3].QProj`, `Layers[0].Experts[2].Up`, `Layers[1].gemma4moe.layerScalar`),
  its layout (the WeightMat's `Kind()`, a plain slice's element type, or `scalar`), its shape, and a SHA-256 over every
  byte and field of it, aikit's unexported WeightMat fields and repacked copies included. It leaves out the config and
  architecture (VK4's program fingerprint covers them), the source handles, the load profile, the k-quant lookup map
  and the `.giw` quant label. A field of a kind it does not know panics.
- **`testdata/loader_identity.json`.** GOARCH → fixture → quant mode → the digest of the fixture's identity lines. The
  fixtures are every committed `testdata/*/` with a `config.json` and a `model.safetensors`, and every committed
  `testdata/*.gguf`. The modes are `parseQuant`'s eight, loaded with the CPU backend's flags. amd64 today: 39
  fixtures, 234 fixture-mode pairs. **The arm64 entry is missing:** a Mac's
  `go test ./decoder/ -run TestLoaderIdentity_table -update` adds it, and until then that test is a named skip on
  arm64, not a pass.
- **`internal/paritykey`.** `LoaderTablePath`, `ReadLoaderTable` and `LoaderDigest(fixtures, table)`, the digest of the
  listed fixtures' entries for every GOARCH and mode. No key reads them (§4).

The four tests take 2.2 s together on this box (`go test -run TestLoaderIdentity -count=1 ./decoder/`).

## 3. Found on the way

- **`SerializeWeights` does not refuse 16-bit weights.** A bf16 or f16 load serializes without error into a CRC-valid
  blob that `LoadSerializedWeights` then refuses ("layer count: blob has 0, arch expects N"), for all 36 safetensors
  fixtures. The entry points refuse those modes before writing a `.giw` (`decoder/stream_dir.go`, `decoder/sidecar.go`),
  so no CLI path reaches it; a caller of the exported function with a `Model` loaded at bf16 would. The reader's
  refusal is clean (an error, not wrong weights). Not fixed here: a writer-side `*SerializeError` is the fix, and it is
  `serialize.go`'s owner's call.
- **Fixtures that share weights.** 24 digests repeat in the table: `cohere-tiny` and `cohere2-tiny`, `gemma2-tiny` and
  `gemma2-hd-tiny`, `qwen3moe-tiny` and `qwen3moe-tiny-k3` load identical tensors, and `glm-tiny.gguf` loads exactly
  what `glm-tiny/` does in f32, int8, int8int8 and int4. That last one is a cross-format check nobody had to write.
- **The identity is per GOARCH** because the int4 repacks are (row4 on arm64, split-half on amd64). Whether two amd64
  runners with different CPU features agree is not yet shown: CI's AVX2 and AVX-512 runners will show it on this PR.

## 4. How it would replace the loader files in the key (DECISION NEEDED)

Today `decoder/weights.go` and `decoder/gguf.go` (the `loaders` set) are in all 43 families' keys. The identity can
stand for them the way VK4's program fingerprints stand for the builders in week 2:

- a family whose fixtures all have entries in the loader table drops `decoder/weights.go` from its key and gains
  `loaders=<LoaderDigest of its fixtures>`;
- it drops `decoder/gguf.go` only if one of its fixtures is a GGUF (today `gemma`, `gemma2` and `glm4_moe`); every
  other family keeps `gguf.go`;
- a family with no fixture with weights keeps both files (the 12 without programs, and `mellum`, whose fixture is a
  config only);
- the fixture list is the family's `programs` list plus its GGUF fixtures, so it needs no new field, or one
  `loader_fixtures` field if VK wants them separate.

The limit is week 2's: a loader change on a path no fixture takes (GPTQ, AWQ, a sharded set, a GGUF quant type no tiny
GGUF holds, MXFP4) leaves every identity unchanged and reads as a refactor. Grading such a week reads each explained
old-only push's loader diff, as week 2 does for builders.

**The decision, with options:**

- **A. A week-3 shadow after week 2 is graded (recommended).** The shadow key gains the loader rule above; week 3
  explains its disagreements by that one change. Pros: the same discipline as weeks 1 and 2, one change per week, and
  week 2's grading untouched. Cons: waits for week 2 to end, and needs the arm64 entry first.
- **B. Fold it into week 2 now.** Pros: a week sooner. Cons: changes the shadow key mid-week, so week 2's disagreements
  no longer have one cause, and its grade starts over.
- **C. Advisory only.** Leave both files in the key, and have `gate shadow` print, for a push that moved a loader file,
  whether every loader identity stayed unchanged. Pros: no key risk. Cons: saves no re-validation.
