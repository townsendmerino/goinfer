## 1. The rule

### 1.1 Design: ABBA blocks of restarted cells

- **A block is one `bench_peer.py` cell per arm**: server restart, one discarded warm-up completion, `BENCH_RUNS` runs ×
  8 completions (3 × 8 by default, as today), teardown. It is the registry's served unit ("one bench_peer.py cell per
  arm (a restart)"), so the registry's sd is a per-block sd without conversion.
- **Blocks come in quads, mirrored:** the engine order is `[e1 … ek]` in the quad's first block and `[ek … e1]` in its
  second (for two arms: A B, then B A). A linear drift across a quad therefore lands on both arms equally. This
  replaces the separate order-reversed pass.
- `BENCH_ABBA=<quads>` in `bench_peer.py` runs a group (model × depth × config) as that many quads before moving on,
  and records each cell's `block` number.

### 1.2 The observation and the look

- Per block b: **d_b = ln(new mean rate) − ln(old mean rate)**, each the mean over that cell's timed completions. For
  a peer gate, "new" is goinfer and "old" the peer.
- **A look after every complete quad** q, over the n = 2q block observations so far.
- **No stop before q = 2** (four cells per arm). v1's disqualifying orders stopped at the second run of a single
  cell. Two quads is the least data in which a level shift in one arm between blocks shows up as spread.
- **The cap Q** is registered per gate, in quads. At the cap the gate's own fixed-N verdict applies (§1.5).

### 1.3 The interval

- **The prior sd σ₀** is the registry entry `power.py` picks for the gate's instrument and cell
  (`lookup(reg, instrument, cell)`): `served-decode` for new ÷ old, `served-decode-peer` for goinfer ÷ peer. **Prior
  df ν₀ = min(entry n − 1, 6)**, and at least 1. A gate whose entry has no sd gets no sequential rule. It runs to its cap.
- **The pooled sd** at a look: s_p² = ((n − 1)·s² + ν₀·σ₀²) / (n − 1 + ν₀), where s is the sample sd of the n block
  observations. So an early look cannot be tighter than the registry says the instrument is, and a run noisier than
  the registry widens its own interval.
- **SE = s_p / √n**, with df = n − 1 + ν₀.
- **Boundaries:** v1's, unchanged:
  - Lan–DeMets O'Brien–Fleming-type spending, α*(t) = 2 − 2Φ(z₀.₉₇₅ / √t), two-sided α = 0.05;
  - t = q / Q, over the looks actually taken (q = 2 … Q);
  - z boundaries by recursive integration, each carried to the t scale at df at the same nominal tail.
- **The floor b₀** is the bias more blocks do not re-draw.
  - It is the entry's `floor` when the entry's provenance is `cross-build-aa` and the gate compares two builds. That is
    the layout/build effect.
  - Otherwise it is 0. ABBA cancels the order effect that the other entries' floors stand for; the registry notes
    that "a counterbalanced (ABBA) design may pass --floor 0".
- **Repeated confidence interval:** I_q = [d̄ − c*_q·SE − b₀, d̄ + c*_q·SE + b₀].

### 1.4 Stopping

Each graded bar pair (ship S, kill K ≤ S) is its own sub-gate:
- lower(I_q) > ln S → stop, **PASS**;
- upper(I_q) < ln K → stop, **non-pass** (FAIL / BEHIND);
- otherwise continue. Inequalities are strict.

### 1.5 The fixed-N verdict (the cap, and the reference the replay is scored against)

This is what a fixed-N gate on all Q quads would say. It is the data-only interval: d̄ ∓ t₀.₉₇₅,ₙ₋₁·s/√n ∓ b₀, with
n = 2Q and no prior pooling, graded as:
- **PASS** if lower > ln S;
- **non-pass** if upper < ln K;
- otherwise **AMBIGUOUS** (parked, as today).

### 1.6 Where v2 applies

Only where `power.py` sizes the gate at more than 4 blocks: a gate that resolves in one or two quads at fixed N has
nothing to save. By the registry today that is:
- nobara CUDA 0.5B cross-build new ÷ old (11 blocks);
- the Mac CPU (9 blocks, or no resolution);
- any gate whose expected effect is near its bar.

## 2. Validation, pre-registered

Two parts, run in this order. **v2 is adopted only if both pass.**

### 2.1 Simulation screen (by day; a screen, not the validation)

Synthetic gates drawn from the registry's noise, plus the heavy-tail events the registry records:
- **per-block noise:** d_b ~ N(ln r, σ₀²). σ₀ takes each of 0.0081 (nobara CUDA), 0.0164 (CUDA 0.5B cross-build,
  floor 0.0144) and 0.0333 (Mac CPU).
- **a level shift:** with probability 0.05 per arm per block, a persistent shift of ±5% in one arm from that block on
  (nobara's recorded cross-session maximum), and with probability 0.01 a ±31% one (peer-claim i's Ollama).
- **true ratios** r ∈ {0.94, 0.97, 1.00, 1.03, 1.06, 1.10}, bars (S, K) ∈ {(0.97, 0.97), (1.03, 0.97)}, Q ∈ {4, 6},
  and 4,000 gates per combination (seed 20260928).

**Pass:**
- P(stop PASS) ≤ 0.05 wherever the true ratio is at or below S. A ratio exactly at S is the boundary case.
- Agreement with the fixed-N verdict ≥ 95% over all simulated gates.
- ≥ 25% fewer blocks than the cap wherever |ln r − ln S| ≥ 2 × (the fixed-N half-width), averaged.

### 2.2 Held-out night corpus on nobara (the validation)

- **Setup:** one night job, `run-te4v2-validation.sh`, ≤ 3 h. Pinned binaries are built before it is queued. It uses
  `BENCH_ABBA`, `BENCH_RUNS=3` and depth 128, runs every gate to its cap, and never stops early. The rule is replayed
  afterwards over the recorded blocks by `te4v2_replay.py`.

| # | gate | arms | cap Q | bar sets graded |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CUDA 0.5B A/A | goinfer vs goinfer_old, the same binary | 6 | NI (S = K = 0.97); peer (S = 1.03, K = 0.97) |
| 2 | CUDA 1.5B A/A | same | 4 | NI; peer |
| 3 | CUDA 7B A/A | same | 4 | NI; peer |
| 4 | CUDA 0.5B vs Ollama | goinfer vs ollama | 4 | NI; peer |
| 5 | CUDA 1.5B vs Ollama | same | 4 | NI; peer |
| 6 | CUDA 7B vs Ollama | same | 4 | NI; peer |
| 7 | CPU 0.5B A/A | goinfer vs goinfer_old, the same binary | 4 | NI; peer |
| 8 | CPU 1.5B vs Ollama | goinfer vs ollama | 4 | NI; peer |

  An A/A has true ratio 1.00: NI should pass, and the peer bars should read LEVEL / AMBIGUOUS, **never** AHEAD.

- **Registry entries:**
  - A/A gates: the `served-decode` entry for the cell. b₀ = 0, since one binary has no build effect.
  - Peer gates: `served-decode-peer`.
- **Scoring:** 16 sub-gates (8 gates × 2 bar sets). The reference is each sub-gate's fixed-N verdict at its cap (§1.5).
- **Pass, all of:**
  1. **In recorded order**, every sub-gate reaches its fixed-N verdict.
  2. **Over quad permutations** (all 24 for Q = 4; 200 uniform ones for Q = 6, seed 20260928), no sub-gate reaches
     PASS in any order where its fixed-N verdict is not PASS. **This is the criterion that killed v1.**
  3. Over those permutations, the mean per-sub-gate agreement is ≥ 95%.
  4. **Savings:** ≥ 25% fewer cells than the caps, summed over the sub-gates in recorded order. The wall-clock saving
     is also reported, from the measured `secs`.
- **Kill:** any of 1–3 fails, or savings < 15%. **Savings of 15–25% → parked**, with the numbers.
- **Few gates, again:** 16 sub-gates means one disagreement is 6%. Criterion 1 is effectively zero disagreements, as
  in v1. Criterion 2 is the one with statistical weight: its permutations are the order hole v1 fell into, now
  measured on data v2 never saw.

### 2.3 What adoption would mean

- `BENCH_SEQUENTIAL=<ship>,<kill>,<cap>` in `bench_peer.py` would stop a group live. It is built only after both parts
  pass.
- Until then `BENCH_ABBA` is available as a design in its own right, a single-pass replacement for the order-reversed
  second pass. Using it is a fixed-N choice and needs no validation beyond the harness's own tests.
