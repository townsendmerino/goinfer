## Two recorder defects, found by the grade (fixed for the next run; this night corrected by hand)

1. **A false pass in the answer check.** When a harness's own reply was empty, `run_cell.py` graded the last 2,000 characters of its raw
   output instead. That text holds session ids and tool output. opencode x Qwen3-4B `read` r1 found no files, gave no answer, and
   passed because a `5` stood alone inside an id. It is graded **fail** below. It is the only answer-graded pass with an empty
   reply. The fallback is removed; replayed on that run's stdout, the old rule passes it and the new one fails it.
2. **No prompt sizes on `/v1/messages`.** A Messages stream carries `input_tokens` only in `message_start`, and `wire_proxy.py` read usage
   from the last 6,000 characters. Every Claude Code request has `prompt_tokens: null`. As a result, `gate_i1.py`'s incurable-jump test
   could not run for Claude Code: with no fixed part known, it is skipped without a warning. The proxy now falls back to the head of
   the body, which a fake stream with `message_start` outside the tail confirms (the pinned proxy records only `output_tokens`). For
   this night the sizes were read from the serve log (`-log-requests`).

follows Mellum2.1's template for the shapes Codex sends. **The check that it is right:** for all 213 fully reused follow-ups, the served

### 2. What the templates do with past reasoning, and where a cut can go without losing reuse

**Mellum2.1's own template keeps all of it inside a tool loop.** It renders the reasoning of every assistant turn after the last real user
query (`loop.index0 > ns.last_query_index`, and tool results do not move that index). A Codex task is one user message, so every turn of
a run keeps its reasoning until a compaction's summary becomes the new query. goinfer applies the same rule (`chat/history.go:qwenAssistant`,
`histMellum21`), pinned against Hugging Face (`chat/mellum21_history_test.go:TestMellum21_templateMatchesHuggingFace`). The other
templates in `chat/` do the same inside a loop. Qwen3 and Qwen3.5 keep reasoning after the last user query. Gemma 4 keeps it after the
last user turn. gpt-oss keeps a call's analysis until a later final answer (`chat/harmony_tools.go`). **None of them drops reasoning
inside a tool loop.** "Keep only the latest" would therefore be a departure from the model's own template, not a fidelity fix. Whether
the model works as well without its earlier reasoning is a quality question. Job D, which replayed no reasoning, scored 8 of 15 against
7 of 15, which says nothing at this n.

**The tension, worked out.** Reuse holds while prompt n+1 starts with the slot's tokens, which are prompt n followed by reply n's
generated ids, compared by token id (`kvmgr` scoring). On this load the windowed-KV ring then allows a divergence 448 positions before
the newest at any point of its cycle, more at some points (one partial reuse here rolled back 475;
`cuda/kvwindow.go:cudaResident.ReusableKV`). Past that, the request is cold.
- **"Drop reasoning from turns older than the last" does not touch only re-prefilled bytes.** Reply n-1's reasoning sits inside prompt n.
  It was prefilled at request n and is in the KV. Dropping it at request n+1 diverges at its start, so the rollback is the rest of
  reply n-1, plus tool output n, plus reply n: a median of **589, over 448 in 122 of 173 steps (71%)**. Nearly every request goes cold.
  The only bytes that are re-prefilled anyway are the new input.
- **Dropping the newest reply's own reasoning** touches only the tail. The rollback is reply n alone: a median of 161, over 448 in 30 of
  173 steps (17%). This is job D's regime. Modelled: 4 compactions but **46 cold continuations**. At about 167 s a compaction pair
  and 100 s a cold prefill, that is about 2,800 s worse than keeping everything.
- **The one cut that keeps reuse whole** is a rule decided by each reply alone, so that it renders the same at every later request: *a
  past reply's reasoning is rendered only if the reply is longer than the rollback.* A short reply's reasoning is dropped at the next
  request with a rollback of at most 448, and a long one's is kept for good. Modelled: **15 → 11 compactions, no cold continuation
  added**. The short replies are 204 of 278 but hold only 22.7% of the reasoning, so the gain is small.
- **With the rollback raised to 1,024**, `kvGrantRollback`'s cap, the same rule with a limit of 1,024 gives **15 → 9, no cold
  continuation added** (those replies hold 36.7% of the reasoning). Dropping everything at that rollback gives 4 compactions with 21
  cold continuations, about even on time. This load raised nothing: no VRAM was left after the 62 expert slots.
- **For scale, an alternative that leaves the reasoning alone:** a 32,768 window for Codex (trigger 28,672) gives **0** modelled
  compactions on these trajectories. Job B's 32K plan fit on this card with the expert cache cut to 52 slots, which costs decode speed.

**PROPOSAL for the owner, not registered.** Add a serve-side rendering rule for `/v1/responses` (and the other replay paths): render a
past reply's reasoning only when the reply is longer than the loaded model's windowed-KV rollback, and keep every reasoning on a model
with no ring. Consider raising the ring's rollback toward 1,024 from expert-cache VRAM; one slot is about 0.1 GB. The gate, registered
before any run:
- **G-a, replay, by day, CPU, minutes.** Re-render all 298 recorded requests from `codex-rollouts.tar.gz` through goinfer's own renderer
  and tokenizer, under keep-all and the rule. Bar 1, reuse kept: under the rule, every plain follow-up's first divergent token is at most
  the rollback before the end of the previous prompt plus the re-tokenized reply. Bar 2, fewer compactions: compactions by the fitted
  Codex trigger (the replay holds trajectories fixed) are at most 0.8 of keep-all's. The model above predicts 11 of 15 (0.73) at the
  448 rollback and 9 of 15 (0.60) at 1,024. Both are inside the bar, so G-a decides whether the replay agrees with the model.
- **G-b, live, at night.** Two arms, keep-all (the do-nothing arm) and the rule, on `mellum-16k-codex-f` at 5 tasks x 3, interleaved by
  task in the same session. Bar: the rule's cold plain continuations are no more than keep-all's. Compactions and the pooled pass rate
  are reported beside, with an ambiguous → parked band for the owner to set. **One pass cannot resolve the compaction effect:** 20
  against 15 is inside the count's own Poisson spread (about ±4.5). The compaction size is therefore G-a's number, and G-b's job is to
  show that reuse and quality do not regress.

### 3. The 15 cold continuations: the windowed-KV floor, exceeded by a reply that does not re-tokenize to its own ids

**What they are not.**
- **Not a request out of turn:** one conversation, one KV slot, runs back to back. The serve log's 300 lines match the records' 298 in
  order, and the two extra lines are the timed-out runs' last requests.
- **Not a client-side edit:** each of the 15 prompts is exactly the previous prompt, plus the re-rendered previous reply, plus the new
  tool output, to within ±5 tokens. Codex inserted no user or developer message (0 in all 15), so the template rendered every earlier
  turn as before.
- **Not an eviction:** there was no other session to evict it.

The first divergence must therefore lie inside the previous reply.

**What they are.** In all 15, the divergence lies more than the rollback before the newest position. The previous replies were 616 to
3,582 tokens long, so the ring could not serve the prefix and returned 0 (`ReusableKV`). Without the ring, each would have reused at
least its 6,617- to 9,955-token previous prompt. The divergence comes from the reply re-tokenizing to ids other than the ones the
model generated. serve compares token ids, and the model's sampled split of a span need not be the tokenizer's canonical one. The
evidence:
- **The counts differ.** In 12 of the 15, the re-rendered reply is a different number of tokens from the generated count (−5 to +1),
  and so are 8 of the 14 partial reuses that had no inserted message. That is 20 of 29, against **0 of the 213 full reuses**.
- **The partial reuses show where.** Their measured divergence points sit inside the previous reply: at escaped quotes and backslashes
  in tool-call arguments (`\"vendor\"`, `'\\bsum\\b'`), at `./...`, at non-ASCII (`edøçin`), at the `</think>` boundary, and in code
  (`(t *testing.) {`).
- **Length drives it.** Replies over 448 tokens diverged in 21 of 49 cases (15 cold, 6 partial). Replies of 448 or less diverged in 8
  of 194 cases, and could not go cold.

**The one other cause:** `edit` r2 request 8 (partial, not cold). Codex inserted a `<codex_internal_context source="goal">` user message
(the goal's token budget). That moved the template's last-query index past the previous reply, so its 290 tokens of reasoning stopped
being rendered: a rollback of 320, inside the floor.

**What is not recorded:** the token ids. serve at this build logs neither the reused length nor the first divergent position, so each
cold case has a bound only: the divergence lies at least 448 before the end of the reply. A reuse line in `-log-requests` would show it
directly. **A second PROPOSAL, not registered:** keep the newest reply's generated ids with the slot, and when the next prompt's
re-rendered reply is byte-identical to their decoded text, use those ids instead of re-tokenizing. That removes this cause at its
source, ring or no ring: here it is 15 cold prefills (1,807 s, 21% of the wall) and 14 partial ones.
