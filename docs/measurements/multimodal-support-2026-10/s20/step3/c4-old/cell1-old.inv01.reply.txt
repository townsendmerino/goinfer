### **Field Analysis**

**Invoice No**
This field provides a unique identification number for this specific transaction. It is used for tracking the order in accounting software and cross-referencing with purchase orders. A clerk should ensure this number is unique and matches the number recorded in their internal system.

**Date**
This records the date the invoice was issued. It serves as the starting point for the payment timeline and helps track when the transaction occurred. A clerk should verify that the date is current and falls within the expected timeframe for the-goods/services provided.

**Due Date**
This field specifies the deadline by which the payment must be received. It is calculated based on the issue date and the "net 30" payment terms. A clerk should use this to prioritize payments and flag any overdue accounts.

**From**
This identifies the seller or service provider issuing the invoice. It provides the legal name and contact address of the vendor. A clerk should verify that the vendor's information matches their approved supplier list.

**Bill To**
This identifies the customer or recipient responsible for paying the invoice. It provides the legal name and billing address of the client. A clerk should ensure the "Bill To" entity matches the client who placed the order.

**No (Item Number)**
This is a sequential number used to organize the line items in the invoice. It helps in referencing specific rows during discussions or audits. A clerk should check that the numbering is logical and follows a sequence.

**Description**
This field provides a text-based explanation of the goods or services being billed. It allows the buyer to understand exactly what they are paying for. A clerk should verify that the descriptions match the items or services actually received.

**Qty (Quantity)**
This records the numerical amount of units, hours, or measurements for each line item. It is the multiplier used to calculate the total cost for that specific row. A clerk should cross-reference this quantity with delivery notes or work logs to ensure accuracy.

**Unit Price**
This indicates the cost for a single unit of the described item or service. It is the base rate before any quantities are applied. A clerk should verify that this price aligns with the agreed-upon contract or quote.

**Amount**
This is the total cost for a specific line item, calculated by multiplying the Quantity by the Unit Price. It provides the total cost for that specific entry. A clerk should perform a quick calculation to ensure the math is correct.

**Subtotal**
This is the sum of all the individual "Amount" fields before taxes or discounts are applied. It represents the raw cost of the transaction. A clerk should verify that the sum of all line items matches this figure.

**Tax (8.5%)**
This records the amount of tax applied to the subtotal based on the specified percentage. It is a mandatory addition to the cost required by law. A clerk should verify that the tax rate used is correct for the jurisdiction of the transaction.

**Total Due**
This is the final amount that the customer is required to pay, including the subtotal and taxes. It is the most critical figure for the accounts receivable department. A clerk should ensure this figure is clearly visible and matches the total of the subtotal plus tax.

**Payment terms**
This describes the conditions under which the payment must be made (e.g., "net 30 days"). It dictates the credit period allowed to the buyer. A clerk should use this to set the expected payment window in their accounting system.
