**Field Descriptions**

**Company Name & Address**
This field identifies the vendor issuing the invoice and provides their contact location. It establishes the legal entity responsible for the products or services listed. A clerk should ensure this matches the vendor's official business registration.

**Invoice #**
This is a unique identification number assigned to this specific billing transaction. It is used for tracking, auditing, and referencing the transaction in accounting software. A clerk should verify this number against purchase orders or internal records to prevent duplicate billing.

**Date**
This records the specific day the invoice was generated. It serves as the starting point for the payment timeline and aging reports. A clerk should check this against the "Due date" to ensure the billing cycle is correct.

**Customer & Customer Address**
This identifies the entity responsible for paying the invoice and where they are located. It links the bill to a specific client profile in the accounting system. A clerk should verify that the customer name and address match the client's current account details.

**Due date**
This indicates the deadline by which the payment must be received. It is calculated based on the "Date" and the "Payment terms" provided at the bottom. A clerk should use this to monitor for late payments and manage cash flow.

**No, Description, Qty, Unit Price, & Amount**
These fields provide a line-item breakdown of the goods or services provided, including the quantity and cost per unit. The "Amount" is the product of "Qty" and "Unit Price" for each line. A clerk should verify that the descriptions match the work performed and that the math for each line is correct.

**Subtotal**
This is the sum of all "Amount" lines before taxes or discounts are applied. It represents the core value of the goods or services provided. A clerk should cross-check this sum against the individual line item totals.

**Tax (8.5%)**
This records the amount of tax applied to the subtotal based on the specified percentage. It is a-calculated field based on the "Subtotal." A clerk should verify that the tax rate applied is correct for the customer's jurisdiction.

**Total Due**
This is the final amount the customer is required to pay, including the subtotal and tax. It is the bottom-line figure used for payment processing. A clerk should ensure this total is mathematically accurate and matches the expected payment amount.

**Payment terms**
This specifies the conditions under which the payment should be made, such as "net 30 days." It dictates the relationship between the "Date" and the "Due date." A clerk should check this to ensure the customer is billed according to their agreed-upon credit terms.
