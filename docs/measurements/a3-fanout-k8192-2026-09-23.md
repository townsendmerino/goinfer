# A3 f32-attention fan-out at K=8192 — measured: the trend does NOT keep climbing

**P23** (`docs/queue-performance.md`): the original A3 work (`docs/measurements/a3-f32-attention-fanout-2026-09-01.md`)
measured 1.40×/1.58×/1.92× at K=1024/2048/4096 and stopped there on purpose — "extrapolating past
measured points is the exact error that same campaign spent a morning retracting elsewhere." This
adds the fourth point.

## Provenance

nobara-pc (Linux/amd64, 16 cores), not the MacBook the original work used — **this is a fresh,
self-contained curve on this box, not a filled-in cell on the Mac's own curve**; the original work's
provenance rules require same-machine before/after comparison, which a cross-box splice would
violate. Model: qwen2.5-coder-0.5b-instruct q4_k_m (same checkpoint/quant as the original), from
`~/models`. Commit `28378a39`. Method unchanged from the original: `TestA3FanoutEndToEnd`
(`decoder/a3_fanout_e2e_test.go`), paired and interleaved through the real caller
(`forwardLayersN`), `GOINFER_PREFILL_ATTN_WORKERS=1` vs unset, bit-identical logits asserted before
any timing, alternating which arm leads. Added K=8192 to the existing `depths` list (was
`{1024,2048,4096}`) — no new harness. Idle box (load 0.01/0.07/0.18 at launch).
`GOINFER_A3_FANOUT=1 go test -v ./decoder/ -run TestA3FanoutEndToEnd -timeout 45m`, 38m31s total,
`--- PASS` (bit-identity held at every depth including the new one).

## Result

| K | pre-A3 | A3 | ratio | pairs |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1024 | 10.63s | 5.04s | 2.11× | 2.11 2.11 2.17 |
| 2048 | 31.85s | 11.00s | 2.90× | 2.89 2.90 2.93 |
| 4096 | 112.37s | 30.13s | **3.73×** | 3.72 3.73 3.73 |
| **8192** | **285.02s** | **91.35s** | **3.12×** | 3.07 3.13 3.13 |

**The trend does not hold to K=8192 — it peaks around K=4096 and recedes.** Tight within each
depth (all three pairs at 8192 land within 2%: 3.07/3.13/3.13), so this is not a single-pair
outlier. On this box the curve is 2.11× → 2.90× → 3.73× → 3.12×, not the naive monotone
extrapolation past 4096 would have predicted (which, read off 1.92×'s own growth rate on the Mac,
would have guessed something well above 3.73×, not a drop).

**Absolute ratios here are higher than the Mac's own numbers at every depth (2.11 vs 1.40 at 1024,
3.73 vs 1.92 at 4096) — expected and not comparable directly.** Different CPU architecture
(amd64 vs arm64), different core count, different `linalg.MatmulBT` codegen. The Mac's own numbers
are not superseded by this; they remain the citable figures for that box. What this DOES answer,
on its own terms, self-consistently: whether the growth-with-K trend this mechanism produces
continues indefinitely or rolls over at some depth — and on this box, it rolls over.

## What was checked and ruled out, not left as a guess

**Hypothesis: `prefillAttnWorkers`'s own scratch-budget ceiling (`decoder/scratch.go`) silently
drops the worker count below `maxAttnWorkers=6` at K=8192, explaining the rollover directly** —
its own doc comment says exactly this shape happens by design ("the worker count falls back toward
serial as the prompt grows rather than the allocation growing without bound"). Computed, not
assumed, for this model's real geometry (nH=14, hd=64, from the GGUF's own
`qwen2.attention.head_count`/`embedding_length` — 896/14): at K=4096, `perSlot` ≈ 12.5 MiB
(with `fusedAttention()` on by default, `FusedAttnKeyBlock=512` included) → `n` ≈ 21, still capped
at 6 by `maxAttnWorkers`. At K=8192, `perSlot` ≈ 14.75 MiB → `n` ≈ 17, **still capped at 6**. The
256 MiB scratch budget does not bind at either depth for this model — **ruled out**, not the
explanation here. Whatever causes the rollover is elsewhere (a genuine Amdahl-shape effect, as the
original doc's own "58% serial" finding would predict at some point, a memory-bandwidth ceiling as
more worker threads compete for the same DRAM streaming the K/V gather, or something not checked
here) — **not established**, reported as open rather than guessed at.

## What this does not claim

- **Not a Mac measurement.** Does not fill the original doc's own "K=8192 is NOT measured here"
  gap on THAT box — that gap is still open, and closing it needs the Mac itself.
- **The mechanism of the rollover is not identified**, only one candidate explanation (worker-count
  ceiling) that was checked directly and excluded.
- **CPU only**, same scope restriction as the original work.
