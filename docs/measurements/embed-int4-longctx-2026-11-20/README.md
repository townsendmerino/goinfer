# The int4 output head at long context, against HF f32 (#1160; registered 2026-11-20)

**Status: REGISTERED 2026-11-20 before any data; RUN and graded the same day (*Results*): 0.5B PASS, 1.5B does not pass.** Owner's go: Francis, 2026-11-20 08:55 PST, relayed by the coordinator.
A bar that turns out wrong gets a dated amendment with its mechanism, not an edit.

**Why.** On CUDA, `--embed-int4` (the default since 2026-09-28) makes the 0.5B's first-token distribution near-flat on a
filler prompt past about 1k tokens. The int8 head does not do that
([`../peer-reanchor-2026-11-19/repro-0.5b-8000/README.md`](../peer-reanchor-2026-11-19/repro-0.5b-8000/README.md), issue
#1160). The int4 head's quality cost is disclosed as "about 2.3 points of top-1", measured before the flip and never
re-run (`docs/quantization.md`). This asks whether that cost holds on real long prompts.

## Design

- **Models:** Qwen2.5-Coder-0.5B-Instruct and 1.5B-Instruct, the Q4_K_M GGUFs in `~/models` that every peer cell loads.
- **Reference:** HF transformers 5.12 (torch 2.12, CPU) in f32 on each GGUF's own weights, dequantized by transformers.
  - So the reference has exactly the weights goinfer is given. What is measured is goinfer's own error: the int4
    requantization and the head. The file's Q4_K_M loss is not part of it.
  - Generated by [`ref.py`](ref.py): one causal forward per document. The full log-softmax at each scored position is
    stored as float16.
- **Arms:** goinfer `serve`-equivalent loads through `decoder.Load`, with `-quant int4` as in the sweep.
  - **A, the candidate:** CUDA resident, the int4 head (`EmbedInt4: true`, the default).
  - **B, the exact arm:** CUDA resident, the int8 head (`EmbedInt4: false`, i.e. `--embed-int4=false`).
  - **C, descriptive:** CPU, the int4 head (the default).
  - Arms A and B use f16 KV, as served, with a resident context of 8320. The resident is driven one position at a time
    (`ResidentForward.Forward`, teacher-forced). Batched prefill is not in the arm, because the head is the same on both
    paths.
- **Documents:** six real documents, each the text at `9d7485a29` (origin/main, 2026-11-20), cut to its first 8,256
  tokens. Teacher-forced as raw text, no chat template.
  - Code: `internal/app/serveapp/openai.go`, `backend/cuda/resident.go`, `decoder/weights.go`.
  - Prose: `docs/server.md`, `docs/env-vars.md`, `RELEASING.md`.
- **Scored positions:** the 64 positions ending at each K = 256, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192, which is 320 a document. K = 256 is
  the short-context control.
- **Metric, per position:**
  - top-1 agreement with the reference's argmax;
  - KL(reference ‖ arm) in nats over the full vocabulary;
  - a hard flip by `decoder.NearTieArgmaxForTest`.

## Decision rule

- **The criterion:** `internal/fidelity.NonInferior`, candidate A against exact B.
  - The cells are the five K. Each (document, K) window is a prompt, which makes 30 prompts a model.
  - Margins: `AgreeMarginPts = 2.5`, the disclosed ~2.3-point cost rounded up; `KLMargin = 0.25`; one-sided alpha 0.05;
    hard flips under the package's pooled bound.
  - The grader is `TestEmbedInt4LongContext_grade` in `backend/cuda/embed_int4_resident_test.go`.
- **PASS** (both models): the int4 head's cost against the int8 head is within the disclosed cost, pooled over 256 to
  8192 tokens.
- **FAIL** (either model, with a point estimate outside a margin): the int4 head costs more than disclosed on real
  prompts. Weighing that against its speed is Francis's call.
- **AMBIGUOUS** (the point estimates are inside the margins, but a bound is outside): not resolved at this N. Francis
  decides whether to extend.
- **Reported beside, not graded:**
  - the per-K values, which show whether the gap grows with context;
  - arm C against the reference.
- **Stopping rule:** a fixed set and no early stop. A VOID arm (a load that is not `cuda-resident`, or a head table that is
  not the one asked for) is fixed and re-run, never graded.
- **No default changes here.**

## Tier and cost

  against an independent reference.
- **Estimated machine time, about 90 minutes (stated before the run):**

### Per K: does the cost grow with context?

The per-cell values, each from 6 prompts, so each is noisy alone.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 256 | 84.38 / 85.16 (−0.78) | 1.215 | 86.98 / 87.76 (−0.78) | 1.177 |
| 1024 | 78.65 / 78.39 (+0.26) | 1.162 | 83.85 / 85.68 (−1.82) | 1.348 |
| 2048 | 81.51 / 81.77 (−0.26) | 1.270 | 83.33 / 87.76 (−4.43) | 1.239 |
| 4096 | 82.55 / 83.85 (−1.30) | 1.231 | 81.77 / 83.07 (−1.30) | 1.175 |
| 8192 | 78.39 / 81.25 (−2.86) | 1.141 | 79.43 / 81.51 (−2.08) | 1.193 |

- **KL:** the int4 head adds about 15–35% to the KL against the reference at every length. The penalty does not grow with
  context.
- **Agreement:** the cost is 0.8 pts at 256 tokens on both models, below the disclosed ~2.3.
  - The 0.5B's largest gap is at 8192 (−2.86).
  - The 1.5B's runs from 1.3 to 4.4 pts from 1024 on.
- **Overall:** it is not a clean trend. The pooled figures above are the registered reading.

### Reading

- **The int4 head's cost on real long prompts is close to its disclosure in size.** The pooled cost against the int8
  head is 1.0 pt on the 0.5B and 2.1 pts on the 1.5B. The disclosure says about 2.3; both are now measured on two models
  against an independent reference.
- **It is not uniform.** The cost is smaller at short context (0.8 pts at 256 on both models) and larger at long context
  (2–4 pts at some K), with a KL penalty of about 1.2× throughout.
- **The 1.5B misses the registered non-inferiority bar.** Its agreement and KL bounds cross the margins, and its hard
  flips exceed the bound.
- **#1160's 8-token reply** is this cost meeting a near-flat distribution on a filler prompt. Nothing here points to a
  different defect.
- **Decision:** whether the default stays `--embed-int4` (its speed is the reason for it) or moves to the int8 head is
  Francis's call. Nothing was changed.
