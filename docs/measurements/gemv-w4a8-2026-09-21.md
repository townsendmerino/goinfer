# gemv_w4a8_fwd (decode o-proj / down-proj): already at its bandwidth bound — a null result

Follows `fused-rms-qkv-2026-09-21.md`. After the QKV / gate-up / `glu_quant` changes, `gemv_w4a8_fwd` was the next-largest kernel on the D7 decode profile (3.16 ms per token, 25.9%, two launches per layer:
o-proj and down-proj). The shipped kernel is aikit's (`gpu.QuantGEMVPTX`, a separate repo), so anything here means a new kernel in a goinfer module and a routing change, not an edit in place.

## Hypothesis, and what it predicted

Per 2x iteration each lane issues 2 DRAM weight loads but also ~6 cache-resident loads (4 activation words, 2 scale halves): a 3:1 ratio of cached to DRAM loads, the LSU-issue-throttling shape the
older split-KV scores kernel had. Three changes keep every lane's accumulation order intact (lane l folds words l, l+32, ... in ascending order with the same `__fmaf_rn` sequence; the final
5-step shuffle reduction is unchanged), hence are bit-identical: **S** stage the int8 activation in shared memory once per block; **H** load each group scale with 16 lanes and broadcast by shuffle;
**C** read weights with `ld.global.cs` (streaming). Plus rows-per-warp {1,2,4} to amortise the staging. Seven variants (`s h c sh sc hc shc`), source in `gemv_w4a8_x-2026-09-21/gemv_w4a8_x.cu.txt`.

## Bit-identity (held; irrelevant to the outcome)

`TestGemvW4A8XBitIdentical`: all 7 variants x rows-per-warp {1,2,4} x both accumulate modes on 12 real decode projection shapes: 1,198,848 outputs, 0 differing bits.

## Result: no lever

`ncu gpu__time_duration.sum`, 15 launches per cell, random int4 weights, RTX 2070 SUPER, idle box. Original kernel:

| shape (N,K) | original us | GB/s of weights | best variant | best / original |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| D7 o (3584,3584) | 20.2 | 318 | hc, rpw 4 | 1.000 |
| **D7 down (3584,18944)** | **93.7** | **362** (81% of the card's peak) | hc, rpw 1 | 0.990 |
| 1.5B o (1536,1536) | 6.0 | 198 | c, rpw 2 | 0.968 |
| 1.5B down (1536,8960) | 23.2 | 297 | hc, rpw 2 | 0.945 |
| 0.5B o (896,896) | 3.7 | 109 | h, rpw 1 | 1.017 (worse) |
| qwen3-4b down (2560,9728) | 36.3 | 343 | hc, rpw 1 | 0.986 |
| llama3-8b down (4096,14336) | 83.3 | 352 | hc, rpw 2 | 0.975 |
| gemma3-1b down (1152,6912) | 13.8 | 288 | h, rpw 1 | 0.970 |

(All twelve shapes are in the raw CSVs.) The best variant on any shape is 1-5% faster and mostly under 2%; on D7 that is about 0.9 us of 93.7 per down launch, **~0.03 ms of a 12.2 ms token (0.2%)**. Variants **H** (shuffled
scales) and **C** (streaming loads) are within +/-3% of the original; **S** (shared-memory activation) is **8-25% SLOWER** everywhere, including with a conflict-free 64-bit shared load (`ncu-shared-int2.csv`; D7 down
still 1.22x), so the two-strided-LDS.32 bank-conflict explanation is ruled out too. The refuted hypothesis: the kernel is not LSU-issue-bound on its cached loads. It is at its bandwidth bound: the large
shapes run at 320-362 GB/s of a ~448 GB/s peak, and the LM head (same stream, int8) at ~420 GB/s.

**Nothing ships and the kernels are not in the tree** (a bound-but-unlaunched pipeline fails the repo's pipeline lint, and 140 KB of PTX would ride in every binary); the source and test are kept as text in the record directory for
reproduction, next to the raw CSVs.

## What this says about the D7 profile

Achieved weight bandwidth per kernel, D7 decode: LM head ~420 GB/s, down-proj GEMV 362, `fused_rms_gu` ~322 (68 MB in 211 us), `fused_rms_qkv` ~250 (8.3 MB in 33 us). The gap that is left is `fused_rms_gu`: same inner loop as the down GEMV
but 10% lower bandwidth, and it is 47% of the token. At the down GEMV's 362 GB/s it would take ~188 us instead of 211 (-23 us x 27 layers = ~0.6 ms, ~5% of a token). Not measured: why it is slower (candidates: its shared-memory activation
reads, its redundant prologue, the gate/up double-buffer layout); the rows-per-warp table already shipped moved it only ~2%. That, not this GEMV, is the remaining lever on this profile.
